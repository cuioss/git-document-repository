Incident response reports are written in Confluence today, under time pressure, by people who just spent a night on
the incident. The 72-hour notification deadline is somewhere in a calendar, the mandatory fields are somewhere in a
template nobody enforces, and next year's question "how often did this root cause recur" is answered by reading
free text. This category replaces the report, not the handling.

== Handling and Report Are Two Things

* *Handling the incident* (alerting, coordination, escalation in real time) does not belong in Git. Chat, ticket
  system, on-call service and Confluence do that.
* *The report on the incident* (course, cause, impact, lessons, measures, notification duties) is a document with
  structure, review and approval, the kind of document the schema-driven approach on Git is made for.

The live phase therefore stays in the chat, the ticket system, the on-call service and Confluence; only the report
moves into CISOs-Friend. Handling the live phase in CISOs-Friend would not work: Git is no real-time medium, and a
commit per chat message would bury the report. The application connects to none of these systems: the report refers
to tickets and to measures (issues) by the ids the user or an agent enters.

== What a Form Gains over Confluence

. *Mandatory fields and deadlines are enforced.* Security incidents carry hard notification deadlines (see
  <<_the_deadline_clock>>). A form with a schema shows the deadlines and checks the mandatory fields; a Confluence page
  cannot.
. *Review and approval* in the application, with step-up for the release, immutable after approval,
  timestamped: evidence for ISO 27001 A.5.24–5.28 (among them 5.27, learning from incidents, and 5.28, collection of
  evidence).
. *Machine-readable*: categories, times and root causes are fields, ready for evaluations across incidents; in
  Confluence they are free text.
. *Links*: an incident leads to risks in the ISMS register and records its measures by issue id.
. *Data protection and confidentiality*: a repository per year with a retention period (`isms-incidents-2026`),
  no personal details in the report (they stay in the ticket system), read access for a small circle
  (link:data-protection-and-repository-layout.adoc[Data Protection and Repository Layout]).

Where Confluence is better: joint live editing and commenting, familiarity for non-technical participants, and it
runs already. That is why the live phase stays there.

== The Deadline Clock

GDPR Art. 33 requires the notification of a personal data breach within 72 hours and the documentation of every such
breach (para. 5). Where it applies, NIS2 requires an early warning within 24 hours and an incident notification within
72 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, an intermediate report on request of the CSIRT or competent
authority, and a final report no later than one month after the notification, or, for an incident still ongoing then,
a progress report at that point and the final report within one month of its handling
(https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj[Directive (EU) 2022/2555], Art. 23(4)). For a trust service provider
the second subparagraph of Art. 23(4) sets the notification itself at 24 hours. NIS2 applies to entities of a type
listed in Annex I or II of the directive that are at least medium-sized enterprises under Recommendation 2003/361/EC
(Art. 2(1)), and to some types regardless of size (Art. 2(2)).

Germany transposes this in https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bsig_2025/__32.html[§ 32 BSIG] (BSI-Gesetz of
2 December 2025, BGBl. 2025 I Nr. 301, in force since 6 December 2025) with the same 24-hour, 72-hour and one-month
deadlines, reported to the joint reporting office of BSI and BBK. One difference: after a progress report, § 32(2)
sets no period for the final report, which is due once the handling is completed; for Germany the month after
resolution is an internal target. The German thresholds in https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bsig_2025/__28.html[§ 28
BSIG] are at least 50 employees, or an annual turnover and a balance sheet total each above EUR 10 million, for the
entity types of Anlagen 1 and 2.

The first deadlines start at a point the report records: the time the organisation became aware of the incident
(`aware-at`). The final NIS2 report runs from the notification that was sent, or, for an incident still ongoing then,
from its resolution (`resolved-at`). From these times and the classification (personal data affected, significant
incident under NIS2) the application computes the due times and shows them in the form and on the dashboard. The rules
are data of the category's workflow, so another period (a trust service provider, a transposition that differs) is a
change of configuration, not of code. The report also records when the incident began (`occurred-at`) and when it was
detected (`detected-at`); neither starts a deadline. Deadlines are computed from the in-memory index; reminders are
runtime state of the application, not persistence. A notification that was sent is a field with its time and its
recipient; a missed deadline stays visible in the released report.

The affected services come from the link:service-catalog.adoc[service catalog]; the protection needs of the incident
are the maximum over their effective protection needs, which already include what the services depend on.

== AsciiDoc Is the Output, Not the Truth

When the form produces AsciiDoc and someone edits that AsciiDoc by hand, the form can no longer load it cleanly.
Hence:

----
incidents/<id>/
  incident.yaml        ← structured fields (schema): times, category, severity, affected services (service:…),
                         notifications, measures
  narrative/*.adoc     ← free-text sections (course, root-cause analysis, lessons) as AsciiDoc fragments
  report.adoc          ← generated: a template joins the YAML and the fragments
----

The structured data stay in YAML; free text may be AsciiDoc with tables and images. `report.adoc` is generated
reproducibly and committed as readable evidence; a CI check that makes sure it matches the YAML is planned, but does
not exist yet. PDF for authorities or
customers is generated from it as well. The mechanism is the library's
link:../../concepts/schema-driven-documents.adoc[schema-driven documents]; this category supplies the schema, the
template and the workflow.

Personal details in the narrative (a named employee who clicked a link) do not go into the report. The narrative names
roles and pseudonyms ("a member of `team:payments`"); the details stay in the ticket, referenced by its id, and the UI
warns against personal details and interview notes in free text. For a personal data breach the report records the
categories and approximate number of data subjects and records, which is what the notification to the supervisory
authority asks for (GDPR Art. 33(3)(a), link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj[Regulation (EU)
2016/679]).

== Existing Alternatives

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Tool |Assessment

|incident.io, FireHydrant, Rootly, PagerDuty
|Commercial SaaS for operational incidents (SRE); strong in the live phase, produce postmortems; the data lie with
the vendor

|Jira Service Management with Confluence templates
|Close to the current setup, but free text without a schema

|Netflix Dispatch
|Open source, live phase with Slack; archived on GitHub (https://github.com/Netflix/dispatch[Dispatch on GitHub])

|DFIR-IRIS
|Open source (LGPL-3.0) incident response platform with report templates
(https://docs.dfir-iris.org/latest/operations/reports/[IRIS reports]); closest for security incidents, but a
PostgreSQL database and a Docker Compose stack of its own (https://github.com/dfir-iris/iris-web[iris-web on GitHub])

|TheHive
|Case management for SOC teams; TheHive 5 needs a licence in every edition, the free Community licence is for
non-commercial use (https://docs.strangebee.com/thehive/installation/licenses/about-licenses/[TheHive licenses])

|Postmortems as Markdown in Git
|Established practice (template of the Google SRE book); shows that the approach carries, but without form, schema and
deadlines
|===

No tool known to this concept structures reports, reviews them, stores them without additional operation and links
them to the ISMS, the threat models and the notification duties. The gap is the same as for SAMM and threat models.
