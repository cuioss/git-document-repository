Incident response reports are written in Confluence today, under time pressure, by people who just spent a night on
the incident. The 72-hour notification deadline is somewhere in a calendar, the mandatory fields are somewhere in a
template nobody enforces, and next year's question "how often did this root cause recur" is answered by reading
free text. This category replaces the report, not the handling.

== Handling and Report Are Two Things

* *Handling the incident* (alerting, coordination, escalation in real time) does not belong in Git. Chat, ticket
  system, on-call service and Confluence do that.
* *The report on the incident* (course, cause, impact, lessons, measures, notification duties) is a document with
  structure, review and approval, the kind of document the schema-driven approach on Git is made for.

The live phase therefore stays in the chat, the ticket system, the on-call service and Confluence; only the report
moves into CISOs-Friend. The application reads the ticket system and the chat to import the timeline into the report
and writes nothing into them; measures are created as issues in the issue tracker and referenced by id. The decision
is recorded in
link:../adr/0008-Incident_handling_stays_in_ticket_and_chat_only_the_report_lives_in_Git.adoc[ADR-0008].

== What a Form Gains over Confluence

. *Mandatory fields and deadlines are enforced.* Security incidents carry hard notification deadlines: GDPR Art. 33
  (notification within 72 hours; documentation of every personal data breach, para. 5) and, where it applies, NIS2
  (early warning within 24 hours and notification within 72 hours of awareness; a final report one month after the
  notification, or, for an incident still being handled then, a progress report at that point and the final report
  one month after it is handled; Art. 23(4)) _(unverified against the national transposition; whether NIS2 applies
  depends on sector and size)_. A form with a schema shows the deadlines and checks the mandatory fields;
  a Confluence page cannot.
. *Review and approval* by pull request, with a passkey approval where configured, immutable after approval,
  timestamped: evidence for ISO 27001 A.5.24–5.28 (among them 5.27, learning from incidents, and 5.28, collection of
  evidence).
. *Machine-readable*: evaluations across all incidents (categories, time to detection and resolution, recurring
  causes); in Confluence that is free text.
. *Links*: an incident creates risks in the ISMS register, measures as issues and, where it applies, a review of the
  threat model of the affected service.
. *Data protection and confidentiality*: a repository per year with a retention period (`isms-incidents-2026`),
  personal details encrypted through the keyring, read access for a small circle
  (link:data-protection-and-repository-layout.adoc[Data Protection and Repository Layout]).

Where Confluence is better: joint live editing and commenting, familiarity for non-technical participants, and it
runs already. That is why the live phase stays there.

== The Deadline Clock

The deadlines start at a point the report records: the time the organisation became aware of the incident (`aware-at`).
From that field and the classification (personal data affected, NIS2 applicable) the application computes the due times
of the notifications and shows them in the form and on the dashboard. The report also records when the incident began
(`occurred-at`; where unknown, the first timeline entry) and when it was detected (`detected-at`); the time to detection
is `detected-at` minus `occurred-at` and starts no deadline. Deadlines are computed from the in-memory index; reminders
are runtime state of the application, not persistence. A notification that was sent is a field with its time and its
recipient; a missed deadline stays visible in the released report.

The affected services come from the service catalog, and with them their protection needs and data classes. The
`dependsOn` graph of the catalog names the services an incident may have reached; the protection needs inherit by the
maximum principle and feed the classification.

== AsciiDoc Is the Output, Not the Truth

When the form produces AsciiDoc and someone edits that AsciiDoc by hand, the form can no longer load it cleanly.
Hence:

----
incidents/<id>/
  incident.yaml        ← structured fields (schema): times, category, severity, affected services (service:…),
                         notifications, measures
  narrative/*.adoc     ← free-text sections (course, root-cause analysis, lessons) as AsciiDoc fragments
  report.adoc          ← generated: a template joins the YAML and the fragments
----

The structured data stay in YAML; free text may be AsciiDoc with tables and images. `report.adoc` is generated
reproducibly and committed as readable evidence; a CI check that makes sure it matches the YAML is planned, but does
not exist yet. PDF for authorities or
customers is generated from it as well. The mechanism is the library's
link:../../concepts/schema-driven-documents.adoc[schema-driven documents]; this category supplies the schema, the
template and the workflow.

Personal details in the narrative (a named employee who clicked a link) are a special case: the form offers a field
encrypted with the data encryption key (DEK) of the data subject, and the UI warns against personal interview notes in
free text.

== Existing Alternatives

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Tool |Kind |Assessment

|incident.io, FireHydrant, Rootly, PagerDuty
|Commercial SaaS, focused on operational incidents (SRE)
|Strong in the live phase with chat integration, produce postmortems; the data lie with the vendor

|Jira Service Management with Confluence templates
|Commercial, Atlassian
|Close to the current setup, but free text without a schema

|Netflix Dispatch
|Open source incident management with Slack
|Live phase; archived on GitHub (last push 2025-09-03) and no longer maintained

|DFIR-IRIS _(unverified)_
|Open source handling of security incidents (forensics), report templates
|Closest for security incidents; a database and operation of its own

|TheHive _(unverified)_
|Platform for security incidents in a SOC
|For SOC teams; commercial licence since v5 to this concept's knowledge

|Postmortems as Markdown in Git
|Established practice (template of the Google SRE book)
|Shows that the approach carries, but without form, schema and deadlines
|===

No tool known to this concept structures reports, reviews them, stores them without additional operation and links
them to the ISMS, the threat models and the notification duties. The gap is the same as for SAMM and threat models.
