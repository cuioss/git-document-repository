Most documents of CISOs-Friend hang on an application or service: its SAMM assessment, its threat model, the incidents
that affected it, the risks it carries. Written as free text, "Payment API", "payments-api" and "the PSP service" end up
in three documents that mean the same thing. The application therefore keeps a catalog of services and refers to its
entries by stable id (`service:payment-api`), the same way the library's directory catalogs refer to roles and teams
(xref:../../concepts/directory-and-catalogs.adoc[Directory and Catalogs]).

== The Catalog Entry

The library ships the catalogs of roles and teams and lets an application declare further ones. CISOs-Friend declares
three: services (one file per entry), systems (the products above the services) and external parties (suppliers,
providers). They lie in the `directory` repository, are widely readable and hold no personal data.

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/services/payment-api.yaml
id: payment-api
name: Payment API
description: Payment processing for the web shop
system: system:webshop                 # optional: the product or system above
lifecycle: active                      # planned | active | deprecated
owner:
  team: team:payments
  role: role:service-owner             # responsibility by role, not by person
repositories:
  - https://git.example/payments/payment-api
dependsOn:
  - service:customer-db
  - external:psp-provider
protection-needs:                      # confidentiality, integrity, availability
  confidentiality: high
  integrity: high
  availability: high
data-classes: [personal, payment]
----

* *Responsibility by team and role.* The directory resolves them to persons; the catalog holds no personal data.
* *Protection needs* per dimension (confidentiality, integrity, availability) in the categories normal, high and very
  high, as BSI-Standard 200-2 defines them (section 8.2,
  https://www.bsi.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/EN/BSI/Grundschutz/International/bsi-standard-2002_en_pdf.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=2[BSI-Standard
  200-2, IT-Grundschutz Methodology, version 1.0]), decide the review interval of a threat model and the
  classification of an incident.
* *`dependsOn`* yields a dependency graph: the effective protection needs of a service are the maximum over the
  service and every service it depends on. BSI-Standard 200-2 (section 8.2.2) also derives protection needs along
  dependencies, but in the other direction: an application whose results another application depends on takes over
  that application's protection needs (see <<_open_questions>>).
* *Retire instead of delete.* An entry gets `retired: true`; old documents keep referring to it.

How the categories use the catalog is shown in xref:cisos-friend-overview.adoc#_four_categories[Overview, Four
Categories].

== Alignment with Backstage

An organisation may already keep such a catalog in Backstage. The Backstage software catalog holds its entries in
descriptor files in the repository of each service, by recommendation named `catalog-info.yaml`, with the kinds
Component, Template, API, Group, User, Resource, System, Domain and Location
(link:https://backstage.io/docs/features/software-catalog/descriptor-format[Backstage: Descriptor Format of Catalog
Entities]). The catalog therefore uses the same terms and, where possible, the same fields (`system`, `lifecycle`,
`owner`, `dependsOn`, the latter in Backstage's spelling). The mapping is not one to one: Backstage names a single owner
entity where this catalog names a team and a role, and its well-known lifecycle values are `experimental`,
`production` and `deprecated` (same source).

Where Backstage exists, an administrator imports its components, systems and groups; the catalog adds only what
Backstage lacks: protection needs, data classes and the owning role. Where nothing exists, the catalog is the source
of truth.

[#_open_questions]
== Open Questions

. Whether the effective protection needs also flow to the services a service depends on, as BSI-Standard 200-2
  (section 8.2.2) derives them, instead of or besides the maximum over the services it depends on.
. Whether service entries are also imported from a CMDB (i-doit, iTop, ServiceNow).
. Whether a change of the protection needs of a service needs a confirmation by the information security officer.
