= Directory and Catalogs
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3
:toc-title: Table of Contents
:sectnums:
:source-highlighter: highlight.js

Every document needs an owner, an assessor or a responsible team. Writing names into documents puts personal data into
the history; writing free text puts "Payments", "payments team" and "Team Payment" into three documents that mean the
same thing. This concept describes the master data every document refers to: catalogs of roles and teams, catalogs an
application adds, and a small directory of persons under pseudonyms, all referenced by stable ids.

== Scope of the First Iteration

* *Roles are names only.* They describe who is responsible or the contact for something; no right is derived from
  them. Approvals and access are not tied to a role of the directory; access stays a matter of the identity provider
  and the application.
* *Hence no staging.* An assignment a user makes is valid at once; there is no pending state and no confirmation by a
  team lead or an administrator. The history of the directory shows who changed what.
* *Roles and teams come from a catalog.* A user picks them; free text is not allowed. A missing entry is requested
  and added to the catalog by a user the application lets change it.

A later iteration may derive rights from roles. Then a self-chosen role must not grant them: assignments of such roles
need a confirmation by a team lead or an administrator, or come from the identity provider, and a workflow counts
confirmed assignments only.

== Layout

The directory gets a repository of its own:

----
directory (repository)
  catalog/roles.yaml            ← catalog of roles, not personal
  catalog/teams.yaml            ← catalog of teams, not personal
  catalog/…                     ← catalogs the application declares, not personal
  persons/<pseudonym>.yaml      ← one entry per person: name, memberships, hash of the login, no issuer, no sub
  erasures.yaml                 ← erased pseudonyms with their date, no person
----

The catalogs are widely readable and hold no personal data. One file per person keeps concurrent saves free of
conflicts. The entries of persons belong to the default person directory `git`; an organisation can give them a
repository of their own with a smaller circle of readers, or plug in its own source instead (see
xref:data-protection.adoc#_the_person_directory[Data Protection, The Person Directory]).

== Catalogs

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/roles.yaml
- id: security-champion
  name: Security Champion
  description: Contact for security in the team
----

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/teams.yaml
- id: payments
  name: Payments
  parent: team:platform          # optional, for hierarchies
----

* *Stable ids instead of names.* Documents refer to `role:security-champion` and `team:payments`; renaming an entry
  breaks no reference.
* *Retire instead of delete.* An entry gets `retired: true`; old documents keep referring to a retired team.
* *Changes are signed saves.* A new or renamed entry is a save through the catalog's workflow, like every other
  document; which users may change a catalog is the application's decision.
* *Further catalogs are configuration.* An application declares its own catalogs (for example of its services) with a
  file, an id prefix and a schema; references of the form `<catalog>:<id>` resolve the same way.

The catalogs are xref:schema-driven-documents.adoc[schema-driven documents]: form, workflow and validation come from
`git-forms`, so they need no mechanism of their own.

== The Directory of Persons

[source,yaml]
----
# persons/<pseudonym>.yaml
subject-hash: 9f2c…e41a          # SHA-256 over issuer and sub, not the OIDC sub; neither is stored
name: Erika Example
memberships:                     # role per team, not global
  - team: team:payments
    role: role:security-champion
  - team: team:payments
    role: role:developer
since: 2026-11-12
confirmed: 2026-11-12            # date of the last confirmation
----

*Memberships are pairs of team and role* instead of separate fields: a person often belongs to several teams with a
different role in each, and assignments almost always ask for "role X in team Y". The whole entry is personal, not
only the name: pseudonym, team and role together often identify a person. Commits and documents therefore carry only
the pseudonym; how the entry is protected and erased is described in xref:data-protection.adoc[Data Protection].

=== First Login

. The login yields the issuer, the `sub` and usually the name. The person directory finds no entry for the hash of
  issuer and `sub`, creates a random pseudonym, held in memory until the entry is saved, and asks the application to
  show the entry form.
. The form is prefilled with the name from the token. Team and role are picked from the catalogs; a missing entry can
  be requested for the catalog.
. Saving is a commit on `main`, signed by the application like every other, without a release workflow; the entry is
  valid at once. A first login interrupted before this step leaves nothing behind.
. Later the user changes the own entry through the same form.

With a person directory for the operator's own source (identity provider, SCIM, LDAP) there is no entry form: names
and memberships come from that source, and the operator maintains them there.

=== Staying Current

Self-entered data age: teams change and nobody updates. Hence:

* where the identity provider delivers groups or departments, they are taken over and prefill the form; SCIM 2.0
  defines a `Group` resource and the `department` attribute of the enterprise user extension
  (link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7643#section-4.2[RFC 7643, sections 4.2 and 4.3]), and Microsoft Entra ID,
  for example, provisions users and groups to an application's SCIM endpoint
  (link:https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/app-provisioning/use-scim-to-provision-users-and-groups[Microsoft
  Entra ID: SCIM provisioning]);
* a periodic confirmation, yearly or on the first login after a reorganisation: "Is your team still right?"

== What It Means for the Architecture

* Documents refer to roles and teams instead of persons, and the directory resolves `<catalog>:<id>` to entries and a
  role in a team to persons, whether or not the identity provider delivers groups.
* Every kind of document that names persons, roles or teams needs it, so it is a shared part, not one kind among
  others: the optional module `git-directory` (see xref:module-structure.adoc[Module Structure]). It is built on
  `git-forms`; `git-directory` also provides the default person directory `git`.

[CAUTION]
====
A self-maintained directory is only as current as its least attentive member. Periodic confirmation and group
import reduce the drift; they do not remove it. That is why no right depends on it in the first iteration.
====

== Open Questions

. Which legal basis does the directory rest on, legitimate interest or employee data protection? Each operator
  settles this with their data protection officer; the library cannot. The German rule on employee data protection
  (link:https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/__26.html[§ 26 BDSG]) is in force, but its general clause in
  § 26(1) sentence 1 is disputed: the CJEU held on 30 March 2023 (C-34/21, _Hauptpersonalrat der Lehrerinnen und
  Lehrer_) that national rules which do not meet the conditions of Art. 88(2)
  link:https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj[GDPR] are no "more specific rules" and
  must be disregarded unless they form a legal basis under another article of the GDPR
  (link:https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-03/cp230054en.pdf[CJEU press release
  No 54/23]). The ruling concerned § 23(1) sentence 1 HDSIG of Hesse, whose wording largely matches § 26(1)
  sentence 1 BDSG; legal commentary concludes that the federal rule is likely to fail the same test, so that
  processing in private employment rests regularly on Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR
  (link:https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/unanwendbarkeit-nationaler-generalklauseln-des-beschaeftigtendatenschutzes[Gleiss
  Lutz, 26 April 2023]).
