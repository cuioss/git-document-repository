= Directory and Catalogs
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3
:toc-title: Table of Contents
:sectnums:
:source-highlighter: highlight.js

Every document needs an owner, an assessor, a responsible team or an affected service. Writing names into documents puts
personal data into the history; writing free text puts "Payments", "payments team" and "Team Payment" into three
documents that mean the same thing. This concept describes the master data every document refers to: the catalogs of
roles, teams, systems, services and external parties, and a small directory of persons under pseudonyms, all
referenced by stable ids.

== Scope of the First Iteration

* *Roles are names only.* They describe who is responsible or the contact for something; no right is derived from
  them. Approvals and access are not tied to a role of the directory.
* *Hence no staging.* An assignment a user makes is valid at once; there is no pending state and no confirmation by a
  team lead or an administrator. The history of the directory shows who changed what.
* *Roles and teams come from a catalog.* A user picks them; free text is not allowed. A missing entry is added to the
  catalog through a review.

A later iteration may derive rights from roles; it then needs a confirmation of assignments (see
<<_later_iterations>>).

== Layout

The directory gets a repository of its own:

----
directory (repository)
  catalog/roles.yaml            ← catalog of roles, not personal, plain text
  catalog/teams.yaml            ← catalog of teams, not personal, plain text
  catalog/systems.yaml          ← catalog of products or systems above the services, not personal, plain text
  catalog/external.yaml         ← catalog of external parties (suppliers, providers), not personal, plain text
  catalog/services/<id>.yaml    ← one file per application or service, not personal, plain text
  persons/<pseudonym>.yaml      ← one entry per person: name, memberships, hash of the login, no issuer, no `sub`
  erasures.yaml                 ← erased pseudonyms with their date, no person
----

The catalogs are widely readable and hold no personal data. One file per person and per service keeps concurrent saves
free of conflicts; the small catalogs of roles, teams, systems and external parties change rarely. The entries of
persons belong to the default person directory `git`; an organisation can give them a repository of their own with a
smaller circle of readers, or plug in its own source instead (see
xref:data-protection.adoc#_the_person_directory[Data Protection, The Person Directory]).

== Catalogs of Roles and Teams

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/roles.yaml
- id: role:security-champion
  name: Security Champion
  description: Contact for security in the team
- id: role:service-owner
  name: Service Owner
  description: Responsible for an application or service
----

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/teams.yaml
- id: team:payments
  name: Payments
  parent: team:platform          # optional, for hierarchies
----

* *Stable ids instead of names.* Documents refer to `role:security-champion` and `team:payments`; renaming an entry
  breaks no reference.
* *Retire instead of delete.* An entry gets `retired: true`; old documents keep referring to a retired team.
* *Changes by review.* A new or renamed entry is a reviewed save, like every other document.

== Catalog of Applications and Services

Most kinds of documents hang on an application or service: its assessments, its threat model, the incidents that
affected it, the risks of the asset. An entry is larger than one of the small catalogs, hence one file per service:

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/services/payment-api.yaml
id: service:payment-api
name: Payment API
description: Payment processing for the web shop
system: system:webshop                 # optional: the product or system above
lifecycle: active                      # planned | active | deprecated | retired
owner:
  team: team:payments
  role: role:service-owner             # responsibility by role, not by person
repositories:
  - https://git.example/payments/payment-api
dependsOn:
  - service:customer-db
  - external:psp-provider              # an entry of the catalog of external parties
protection-needs:                      # confidentiality, integrity, availability
  confidentiality: high
  integrity: high
  availability: high
data-classes: [personal, payment]
----

Fields taken from Backstage keep its spelling (`dependsOn`); the fields of the own catalog are written in kebab-case
(`protection-needs`, `data-classes`).

* *Responsibility by team and role.* The directory resolves them to persons; the catalog itself holds no personal data.
* *Protection needs* (confidentiality, integrity, availability, as in BSI IT-Grundschutz) decide, for example, review
  intervals and the classification of an incident.
* *`repositories`* links catalog and code: documents kept beside the code, detection of outdated documents, later
  SBOMs.
* *`dependsOn`* yields a dependency graph: the affected services of an incident follow from it, and protection needs
  inherit by the maximum principle.
* *Maintenance.* The owning team keeps the entry through a form; repositories can be proposed from the Git server; a
  service without a repository is allowed (a bought SaaS application).

=== Alignment with Backstage and Import

Many development organisations already keep such a catalog. The Backstage software catalog holds its entries in
descriptor files in the repository of each service, by recommendation named `catalog-info.yaml`, with the kinds
Component, Template, API, Group, User, Resource, System, Domain and Location
(link:https://backstage.io/docs/features/software-catalog/descriptor-format[Backstage: Descriptor Format of Catalog
Entities]); the principle "the description lives in the service's repository" matches the one file per
service of this catalog. Operations often keep a CMDB (i-doit, iTop, ServiceNow); whether entries are imported from
it is an open question. Therefore:

* *The schema leans on Backstage*: the same terms and, where possible, the same fields (`system`, `lifecycle`,
  `owner`, `dependsOn`), so that entries can be mapped. The mapping is not one to one: Backstage names a single owner
  entity where this catalog names a team and a role, and its lifecycle values differ: Backstage names the well-known
  values `experimental`, `production` and `deprecated` and accepts any other value (same source); this catalog uses
  `planned`, `active`, `deprecated` and `retired`.
* *Import*: where Backstage exists, components and systems are imported and kept in sync; groups map onto the team
  catalog. The own catalog adds only what is missing: protection needs, data classes, links to security documents.
* *Where nothing exists*, the own catalog is the source of truth.
* Whether the additional fields stand in `catalog-info.yaml` itself (as annotations) or in the central catalog is
  configurable per organisation.

== The Directory of Persons

[source,yaml]
----
# persons/<pseudonym>.yaml
subject: 9f2c…e41a               # SHA-256 over issuer and sub; neither is stored
name: Erika Example
memberships:                     # role per team, not global
  - team: team:payments
    role: role:security-champion
  - team: team:payments
    role: role:developer
since: 2026-11-12
----

*Memberships are pairs of team and role* instead of separate fields: a person often belongs to several teams with a
different role in each, and assignments almost always ask for "role X in team Y".

=== First Login

. The login yields the issuer, the `sub` and usually the name. The person directory finds no entry for the hash of
  issuer and `sub`, creates a random pseudonym and asks the application to show the entry form.
. The form is prefilled with the name from the token. Team and role are picked from the catalogs; a missing entry can
  be requested for the catalog.
. Saving is a commit, signed by the application like every other (see xref:identity-and-signing.adoc[Identity and
  Signing]); the entry is valid at once.
. Later the user changes the own entry through the same form.

The catalogs are xref:schema-driven-documents.adoc[schema-driven documents]: form, workflow and validation come from
`git-forms`, so they need no mechanism of their own. The entries of persons use the same form and schema, but they are
saved directly on `main`, without a release workflow, and no output is generated for them.

With a person directory for the operator's own source (identity provider, SCIM, LDAP) there is no entry form: names
and memberships come from that source, and the operator maintains them there.

=== Staying Current

Self-entered data age: teams change and nobody updates. Hence:

* where the identity provider delivers groups or departments, they are taken over and prefill the form; SCIM 2.0
  defines a `Group` resource and the `department` attribute of the enterprise user extension
  (link:https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7643#section-4.2[RFC 7643, sections 4.2 and 4.3]), and Microsoft Entra ID,
  for example, provisions users and groups to an application's SCIM endpoint
  (link:https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/app-provisioning/use-scim-to-provision-users-and-groups[Microsoft
  Entra ID: SCIM provisioning]);
* a periodic confirmation, yearly or on the first login after a reorganisation: "Is your team still right?"

=== Data Protection of the Directory

* *The whole entry is personal*, not only the name: pseudonym, team and role together often identify a person ("CISO
  in team X"). Commits and documents therefore carry only the pseudonym; the entry is the one place that links it to
  the person, and the catalogs are not personal.
* *Data minimisation*: name, team and role only. Phone or e-mail only where they are really needed; the e-mail comes
  from the token anyway.
* *Information duty.* The entry form carries the information text the operator supplies (GDPR Art. 13): what the
  directory is for and how long it is kept.
* *Leaving*: the entry is deleted in a commit, and the history keeps its earlier versions (see
  xref:data-protection.adoc#_erasing_a_person[Data Protection, Erasing a Person]). Documents keep the pseudonym and
  show "former member"; because responsibilities in documents run through roles (`owner: role:…`), they stay
  understandable.

== What It Means for the Architecture

* The directory closes a gap: documents refer to roles instead of persons, and the directory resolves `role:`,
  `team:`, `system:`, `service:` and `external:` to persons and entries, whether or not the identity provider delivers
  groups.
* Every kind of document needs it, so it is a shared part, not one kind among others.
* Persons, roles, teams and services are free of any domain, and the directory builds on `git-store` and
  `git-forms`; it is therefore the module `git-directory`
  (see xref:module-structure.adoc[Module Structure]), which also provides the default person directory `git`.
* Which catalogs exist is configuration; further catalogs (suppliers, locations) cost little.

== Later Iterations

* *Rights from roles.* When approvals are tied to roles, a self-chosen role must not grant them: assignments of such
  roles need a confirmation by a team lead or an administrator (a pending state and step-up), or come from
  the identity provider. A workflow then counts confirmed assignments only.
* *Confirmed protection needs.* Changes of the protection needs of a service, on which review intervals and
  notification duties depend, may need a confirmation by the information security officer.
* *Two-way synchronisation with Backstage*, writing the additional fields back as annotations.

[CAUTION]
====
A self-maintained directory is only as current as its least attentive member. Periodic confirmation and group
import reduce the drift; they do not remove it. That is why no right depends on it in the first iteration.
====

== Open Questions

. Align the schema of the service catalog with the Backstage descriptor format and prototype the import.
. Decide whether service entries are also imported from a CMDB (i-doit, iTop, ServiceNow).
. The legal basis of the directory (legitimate interest or employee data protection) is agreed by each operator with
  their data protection officer. The German rule on employee data protection
  (link:https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/__26.html[§ 26 BDSG]) is in force, but its general clause in
  § 26(1) sentence 1 is disputed: the CJEU held on 30 March 2023 (C-34/21, _Hauptpersonalrat der Lehrerinnen und
  Lehrer_) that national rules which do not meet the conditions of Art. 88(2) GDPR are no "more specific rules" and
  must be disregarded unless they form a legal basis under another article of the GDPR
  (link:https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2023-03/cp230054en.pdf[CJEU press release
  No 54/23]). The ruling concerned § 23(1) sentence 1 HDSIG of Hesse, whose wording largely matches § 26(1)
  sentence 1 BDSG; legal commentary concludes that the federal rule is likely to fail the same test, so that
  processing in private employment rests regularly on Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR
  (link:https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/unanwendbarkeit-nationaler-generalklauseln-des-beschaeftigtendatenschutzes[Gleiss
  Lutz, 26 April 2023]).
